# TechWomenAZ platform — feature guide

A plain-language tour of what the platform does today. Written for nonprofit staff; no technical background needed. Everything described here is live in the build — nothing on this list is planned or promised.

## For members

**Joining.** Apply at **Join** as a mentor, a mentee, or both. You can share what you are studying or do for work, what brings you to TechWomenAZ (networking, learning AI, finding a mentor, volunteering and more), and whether you would like community emails. Your application stays private until staff approve it.

**Signing in.** You sign in with a personal, single-use link sent to your own email — there are no shared passwords. Until email delivery is switched on, staff can honestly see that sign-in links are unavailable rather than pretending one was sent.

**Your profile.** You control what other members can see: your headline, bio, expertise, goals, timezone, weekly availability, and whether you prefer to meet online, in person, or either. You also say how many mentees or mentors you can take on, and nothing is suggested to you beyond that capacity.

**Finding a match.** Discovery only shows people whose schedules truly overlap with yours in your own timezone, whose meeting format is compatible, and who have room for another match. You choose: **Interested** or **Pass**. Nothing is sent to anyone when you pass, and passes stay private. When you are both interested, a match is created and a coordinator gives it a final look before it becomes active — this keeps the program safe without slowing anyone down.

**Your relationship.** Each match has a shared goal, a next meeting time, and a meeting link if you use one. After each session you record a quick check-in: did you meet, was it useful, do you want to continue. Your private notes in a check-in are visible only to you — not to the other person, not to other members, not even to administrators.

## For staff

**Applications and members.** Review applications, approve or pause members, and see the full picture of the community — including the new major, career and interest details people share.

**Imports.** Upload the existing Google Forms spreadsheet as CSV. Every row is previewed first and sorted into new, duplicate or invalid, and committing the same file twice never creates duplicates.

**Mentorship oversight.** See all matches, approve ones that came from mutual interest, and watch overall activity. Approvals only apply to matches that are actually waiting for review.

**Website editing.** Edit the public site section by section with a preview, publish when ready, or roll back to any earlier version. AI can suggest draft text, but nothing AI touches is published automatically, and AI suggestions cannot insert code or links.

**Newsletters.** Draft newsletters with optional AI help, and export a list of only the members who explicitly asked for emails, ready to paste into HubSpot. HubSpot remains the sender, so its own opt-out rules still apply.

**Events.** Post community events with registration links; they appear on the public events page.

**Honest status page.** The settings page shows plainly which integrations are connected (database, Jev, Workers AI, email, HubSpot, Turnstile) — an integration that is not configured is reported as not configured, never faked.

## What the platform deliberately does not do

- It never sends automated email campaigns or subscribes anyone without their explicit yes.
- It never publishes AI-written content without staff review.
- It never exposes one member's notes, pass decisions, or contact details to another member.
- Demo data is always labeled as synthetic, and real member counts come from the database, not from marketing copy.
